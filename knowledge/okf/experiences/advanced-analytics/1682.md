Advanced Analytics for A/B Testing - Bayesian Methods & Statistical Decision Making

1. Bayesian A/B Testing Methodology: Prior selection should be weakly informative (e.g., Beta(1,1) for conversions or Half-Cauchy for deltas) to protect against outliers on small samples without pulling estimates. Posterior distribution gives full uncertainty picture; use posterior probability of superiority (P(Δ > 0 | data)) or expected loss for business-value decisions instead of arbitrary 0.95 thresholds.

2. Statistical vs Practical Significance: p-value only tells if effect exists, not its magnitude. Always examine confidence interval for difference and its lower bound relative to MDE (minimum detectable effect). Practical significance only when entire CI lies above business-profitability threshold (e.g., +1% revenue), otherwise even "significant" result is useless for deployment.

3. Sequential Testing & Early Stopping: Classical p-values invalid with peeking. Use alpha-spending methods (O'Brien-Fleming) or always-valid p-values based on martingales. In practice, implement Bayesian sequential analysis with posterior updating after each user; stop when probability of practical win exceeds 0.95 or expected losses < experiment cost.

4. Multiple Comparison Corrections: Bonferroni and Benjamini-Hochberg adjust p-values but lose power. Better: Bayesian hierarchical models with partial pooling that shrink all variant estimates toward common mean, automatically penalizing extreme outliers among many groups. More stable rankings without sensitivity loss, especially with 5-10+ variants.

5. Power Analysis & Sample Size: Classical calculation based on MDE, 80% power, 5% alpha. Bayesian approach: compute data volume needed for 95% posterior interval of difference to exclude zero/threshold given prior dispersion. Always add 20-30% buffer for traffic imbalance, bot filtering, daily patterns; recalculate post-hoc after test ends.

6. Common A/B Testing Pitfalls: Peeking without corrections inflates false positives - forbid looking at p-values until final sample, or use always-valid methods. Simpson's paradox cured by stratification (platform, region, device). Novelty effect cured by excluding first hours/days or pre-period analysis to separate adaptation from true change.

7. Production Implementation: Deterministic user-level randomization (hash user_id with salt), uniform over time to avoid cohort bias. Select ONE primary metric (OEC: LTV or target conversion) and 3-5 secondary metrics tracked informally for hypothesis generation only.

8. Tools & Libraries: Python: PyMC (Beta-Binomial for conversions, LogNormal for revenue, pm.sample() for posterior, compute superiority probability from traces). R: brms (Stan interface, auto-priors, hypothesis() for inequality checks). Classical: statsmodels.stats.power, scipy.stats.ttest_ind with Welch correction.
